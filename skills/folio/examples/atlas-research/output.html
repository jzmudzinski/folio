Emergent capabilities in large language models

A literature review (2022–2026): when and why LLMs exhibit capabilities absent from smaller models. Critical analysis of whether "emergence" is a metric artifact or a real phase transition.

Abstract

The term emergent capabilities (Wei et al., 2022) describes abilities that appear discontinuously with model scale — absent below a compute/parameter threshold, present above. This review surveys 14 studies from 2022–2026 and identifies three competing hypotheses: (1) a real phase transition in representational geometry, (2) an artifact of discontinuous evaluation metrics (Schaeffer et al., 2023), (3) heterogeneous composition of sub-skills already present at smaller scale.

Methodology

14 papers were selected meeting these criteria: (a) empirical evaluation across at least three model sizes from one family, (b) publicly available metrics, (c) a clear emergence criterion (accuracy threshold, BIG-Bench-Hard, etc.).

  • Sources: arXiv (2022–2026), TMLR, NeurIPS proceedings, ICLR proceedings
  • Excluded: blog posts without peer-review, papers without a smaller-model control
  • Quantitative analysis: 8 papers. Qualitative analysis: 6 papers.

Findings

Hypothesis 1 — Phase transition (cited 6×)

Strongest support: Power et al. (2022) — grokking as an analogy to phase transitions in statistical physics; representations move from fragmentary to compositional. Counter-argument: the same curve can be obtained via interpolation if the model is evaluated on a task with a discontinuous criterion (binary accuracy).

Hypothesis 2 — Metric artifact (cited 8×)

Schaeffer, Miranda, & Koyejo (2023) show that emergence disappears when exact-match accuracy is replaced with token-smoothed log-likelihood. Conclusion: capabilities grow smoothly, but the metric "crosses a visibility threshold" only at sufficient signal-to-noise. Counter-argument: some capabilities (e.g. chain-of-thought reasoning) show discrete thresholds even under continuous metrics (Suzgun et al., 2023).

Hypothesis 3 — Compositional sub-skills (cited 4×)

Olsson et al. (2022) — induction heads as a concrete mechanism: a single sub-skill (in-context copy) becomes available to higher-level abilities once the rest of the network is "sufficiently shaped". Fits the observation that emergence shows up in some tasks but not others.

Discussion

None of the three hypotheses explains all the data. The most parsimonious interpretation: emergence is heterogeneous — different capabilities have different mechanisms. Some (arithmetic) show a real phase transition in geometry. Others (multi-step reasoning) are compositional sub-skill unlocks. Many "emergent" capabilities disappear when the metric is fixed.

Verdict

"Emergent capabilities" is an umbrella term hiding at least three distinct phenomena. Suggestion: disaggregate the discussion into (1) sharp phase transitions, (2) compositional unlocking, (3) metric-driven artifacts. Future work should identify which mechanism drives which capability.

Open questions

  • Does heterogeneous sub-skill composition match induction heads for every task, or only in-context learning?
  • How do we measure a "phase transition" in representational geometry independently of the task's metric?
  • Are emergent capabilities voltage-like (vanish below threshold) or capacity-like (gradual but unmeasurable below)?
